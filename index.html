Alap— no fixed tempo yet

Why yoga and altitude belong in the same sentence

Rishikesh sits where the Ganga leaves the mountains and becomes a plain — which is also roughly where most yoga practice stops. You learn to lengthen the breath on a mat, in a room, at sea-level ease. Altitude asks the same question the practice does, just with less patience.

Above 3,000 metres, the air thins enough that breath stops being automatic. You notice it the way you're taught to notice it in pranayama — except now a ridge line is waiting on the other side of noticing. That's the shape of this journey: a week finding your breath on the ghats, then days spent proving it on a trail.

Antara— the rise, the part that names the whole piece

The Yoga + Himalayan Trek Immersion

The first half is grounded in Rishikesh: morning asana and pranayama on the river, meditation, and a slower rhythm to the day than most guests arrive with. The second half leaves the ashram behind for a guided ascent into the Garhwal Himalaya — camping above the treeline, walking mornings, and a summit push timed to weather rather than a spreadsheet.

No trekking experience is assumed. The practice in week one is, in part, training for week two.
