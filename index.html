Why Rishikesh, and why more than asana

Rishikesh has been a seat of practice for longer than yoga has been a wellness word. The ghats where you'll sit each morning are the same stretch of river people have come to for centuries — the postures were never separate from the spiritual work underneath them.

A studio class can teach you the shapes. It rarely has room for the rest — the chanting, the fire ceremony, the stretch of stillness that pranayama is actually pointing toward. That's the gap this immersion is built to close: the physical practice and the spiritual one, taught as one thing, not two.

Antara— the rise, the part that names the whole piece

The Yoga & Spiritual Immersion

Mornings open on the river: asana and pranayama as the ghats wake up, then a slower rhythm to the day than most guests arrive with. Afternoons hold meditation, sound healing, and time simply spent doing less. Evenings gather for the Ganga aarti, the fire and bells rolling out over the water.

Nothing here rushes toward a summit. The rise is inward — from a practice you perform to one you inhabit, by the river.

Katha— the stories along the way

Two stops worth knowing before you go

The outings aren't filler — each one carries its own history. A little context on two of the places the group visits.

Outings · half-day trip

The Beatles Ashram

In 1968, the Beatles came to Rishikesh to study meditation under Maharishi Mahesh Yogi and ended up writing much of what became the White Album. The ashram — officially Chaurasi Kutia — sits inside Rajaji National Park, its old meditation huts and lecture halls now half-reclaimed by the forest and covered in murals left by visitors over the years. Less a monument than a quiet, overgrown reminder of why people have been coming here to sit still for a long time.

Outings · sunrise visit

Kunjapuri Temple

A Shakti temple set on a ridge above the Ganga valley, roughly forty minutes from Rishikesh. Groups leave before dawn to reach it for sunrise, when the Himalayan range shows along the horizon and the town below is still asleep. A steep, quiet climb rewarded with one of the widest views in the region — a different register of stillness than the riverside practice, and one of the outings held for every batch.

Prashna— the questions that come first

What people ask before they come

The practical answers, in one place. Anything not covered here, write to us and we'll answer properly.

Where is the retreat held?

On the ghats of Rishikesh in Uttarakhand, northern India — the stretch of the Ganga at the foot of the Himalayas. Practice runs riverside and on rooftop terraces above the water, with outings into the surrounding valley.

What does a typical day look like?

Two practice sessions daily. Mornings open with asana and pranayama by the river as the ghats wake up. Afternoons hold meditation, sound healing, and unstructured time. Evenings gather for the Ganga aarti, the fire and bells rolling out over the water.

Do I need previous yoga experience?

No prior retreat experience is needed. Sessions are led by Rishikesh-trained instructors and are built for practitioners who came for a class and are ready for a practice — the physical work and the spiritual work taught as one thing, not two.

How many people are in each group?

Eight to twelve guests per batch. Groups are kept small deliberately, so ceremonies are held for the group rather than performed for an audience.

What food is served?

Sattvic vegetarian meals cooked in the ashram tradition — the same food that carries you through the day's practice. Vegetarian throughout.

What is included besides yoga?

Meditation and sound healing, a traditional welcome ceremony, the Ganga aarti, half-day outings to the Beatles Ashram and Patna waterfall, a sunrise visit to Kunjapuri Temple, white-water rafting on the Ganga, and evening sessions of dance, games and storytelling.

Do you also run Himalayan treks?

Yes — alongside the Rishikesh immersion, Antara leads multi-day treks into the Garhwal Himalaya, to modest summits in the 3,000–3,800m range. It can be run on its own or added on to the yoga week. How do I get to Rishikesh?

The nearest airport is Dehradun (Jolly Grant), roughly 20 km away. The nearest major railhead is Haridwar, roughly 25 km away. Rishikesh is about 240 km north of Delhi by road.
